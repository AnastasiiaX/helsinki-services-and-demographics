Services and demographics across Helsinki

How does the availability of everyday public and commercial services across Helsinki's postal areas and districts line up with residents' income and education — and what would it take to attract and keep high-earning, highly educated residents?

Findings

  • Service density falls sharply from the centre outward. Services per capita range from 0.044 in the best-served postal areas (00160 and 00170) down to 0.004 at the low end — an elevenfold spread across the city's 84 postal areas.
  • The same districts lead on income and education. Kaivopuisto, Marjaniemi, Eira, Kaartinkaupunki and Kuusisaari rank highest on median salary against share of educated residents; Jakomäki, Kontula and Itäkeskus rank lowest on both.
  • Everyday retail follows the same gradient, roughly 2:1. Ullanlinna has 1.76 grocery stores per 1,000 residents and Kampinmalmi 1.69, against 0.82 in Mellunkylä and 0.59 in Vuosaari.
  • But the pattern is not uniform, and that is the interesting part. Jakomäki — among the lowest-income districts — has the second-highest pharmacy density at 1.74 per 10,000 residents, ahead of affluent Munkkiniemi and Ullanlinna. Vuosaari, not the lowest-income district, is the most underserved at 0.25. Affluent Munkkiniemi has the most crowded daycare centres of the six districts studied, at about 86 children each. Service provision does not track income cleanly.
